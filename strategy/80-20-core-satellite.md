## Summary

Put the next 10 monthly contributions (about 9,650 CHF) entirely into core, then split every 1,000 CHF as 800 core and 200 satellites. This reaches 80/20 without selling anything.

- **Goal:** long-term growth, 10+ year horizon
- **Contribution:** 1,000 CHF per month
- **Target mix:** 80% core, 20% satellites
- **Tax residence:** Switzerland

## Phase 2: steady state (from month 11)

1. Buy VWCE with 800 CHF every month.
2. Collect the 200 CHF satellite money and invest it once a quarter (~600 CHF).
3. Spend it on the satellite with the most conviction that is still within its caps.
4. No idea fits the caps → the money goes to VWCE.

## Rules (review once a year, e.g. January)

| Situation | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Satellites above 25% | Stop satellite buys; all 1,000 CHF to VWCE until back at 20% |
| Satellites below 15% | Optional: up to 400 CHF a month to satellites until back at 20% |
| A single stock above 5% | No new money into it |
| A single stock above 10% | Consider trimming back to 5%, after checking fees |
| A theme (e.g. silver) above 10% | No new money into that theme |

Rebalance with new contributions first; selling is the last resort.

## Swiss tax points (general rules; confirm for my canton)

- **Capital gains:** generally tax-free for private investors, so trimming a winner usually costs only trading fees.
- **Dividends and fund income:** taxed as income, including income reinvested in accumulating funds like VWCE. Portfolio counts toward wealth tax.
- **US withholding tax:** on US stocks and US-domiciled funds (AMD, IGF, XMMO, SLVP), the 15% withheld is credited via form DA-1 (with W-8BEN at the broker). Inside Irish funds like VWCE, the 15% on US dividends is lost: roughly 0.1% a year on top of the TER.
- **US estate tax:** under the US–Swiss estate tax treaty, Swiss residents get the same exemption as US citizens (USD 15 million in 2026). Heirs must still file Form 706-NA if US assets exceed USD 60,000.
- **Stamp duty:** a Swiss broker charges 0.15% on purchases of foreign securities; a foreign broker does not.
- **Currency:** every contribution is converted to EUR/USD; check the broker's FX fee.
- **Emergency fund:** separate cash so this portfolio never has to be sold in a crash.
