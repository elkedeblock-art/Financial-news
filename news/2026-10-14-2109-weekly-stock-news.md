# Weekly stock market news - 14 October 2026

_Most weekly wraps I found end on 9 October; later figures run to 13–14 October and are labelled by date._

## Headline
- Bond yields dominated: the US 10-year Treasury yield hit its highest level since 2002 in early October, then eased back to around 5.2%.
- Inflation pressure is back in focus, with traders betting the Fed will keep raising rates even if it pauses in October.
- Chipmakers are the weak spot in a market still near record highs, as AI-linked shares sold off again.

## Switzerland
- The SMI closed at 13,637.00 on 8 October and 13,927.52 on 13 October. A MarketScreener snapshot on 14 October showed a five-day gain of about 1.6%, but I could not confirm a full-week close for 7 October.
- Givaudan's Q3 group sales rose and beat expectations on strength in fragrances. Lonza's Q3 was in line and its FY26 outlook was affirmed.
- Roche and Novartis gained on 12 October. The IMF expects Swiss growth of 1.9% in 2026.
- SNB: at its most recent decision in my sources, it held rates at 0%, raised its conditional inflation projections for 2026–2028, and said inflation had risen since June, mainly due to energy. There was no new SNB decision this week.
- EUR/CHF and USD/CHF levels were not confirmed in my sources.

## Europe
- In the week to 9 October, Germany's DAX fell 0.57%, France's CAC 40 fell 1.19% and Italy's FTSE MIB fell 1.46%. Renewed Middle East tensions and a more hawkish ECB tone weighed.
- The Euro Stoxx 50 closed at 6,164.65 on 13 October versus 6,180.29 on 7 October, a fall of about 0.3% (my calculation from Investing.com closes).
- I could not confirm a weekly move for the STOXX 600. Sources describe the ECB as more hawkish but give no specific decision for this week.
- European bourses were lifted midday on 14 October by earnings and stable bond yields.

## United States
- In the week to 9 October, the S&P 500 rose about 1.1–1.2% to close at 7,811.54. The Nasdaq Composite gained 0.64% to 27,366.17 and the Dow rose 0.93% to 51,654.95.
- On 13 October, the S&P 500 closed at 7,805.45 and the Nasdaq at 27,204.38.
- Earnings: big bank results were mixed, and Delta's Q3 disappointed. FactSet expects S&P 500 Q3 earnings growth of 29.6%, which would be a fourth straight quarter above 25%.
- Fed: markets see only a low chance of an October hike but a high probability of another move by December. A hot inflation report reinforced those expectations.

## Rates, currencies and commodities
- The 10-year Treasury yield peaked near 5.37% on 7 October, was about 5.24% on 9 October, and was around 5.19–5.22% on 13–14 October. Sources differ slightly on the exact level.
- The dollar index neared 102.3, its highest since April 2025. Oil prices were elevated: Brent was about $104 and WTI $91.20 on 9 October, before easing on 13 October amid Strait of Hormuz supply risk.
- Gold closed at $4,216.30 on 9 October, up 1.43%, after bouncing from a two-month low. Bank of America says gold needs a dovish Fed to reverse its downtrend.
- Asia: not confirmed in my sources.

## How the market is evolving
- Rates: markets are still pricing a higher-for-longer rate path, and the inflation picture is weakening the case for quick cuts.
- Earnings: the expected 29.6% growth is strong, but it leans heavily on the AI data-centre buildout, so concentration risk is rising.
- Valuations: the S&P 500 forward P/E of 19.1 is in line with its 10-year average and below its 5-year average of 19.8.
- Rotation: smaller companies lagged, with the Russell 2000 down 0.9% in the latest week. Bank stocks have also underperformed, with the KBW Bank Index down 8.4% over three months.
- Disagreement: Bloomberg stresses the chip rout and rate-hike bets, while Fidelity and T. Rowe Price stress market resilience. Sources also differ on the exact yield and oil levels.

## Sector pulse
- **Technology and chips lost favour.** Chipmakers fell again and Micron dropped 3.9% on 12 October amid competition from China's CXMT. Case: AI infrastructure drives strong earnings growth. Risk: concentration, competition and bond stress at AI cloud firm CoreWeave.
- **Healthcare and real estate led the S&P 500 on 9 October**, though this was a single session. Swiss pharma Roche and Novartis also gained. Case: relatively defensive exposure. Risk: one-day moves may not signal a trend.
- **Banks lagged.** Case: higher yields can support net interest income. Risk: mixed results and a weak three-month share price trend.
- **Companies to research further:** Givaudan (case: a Q3 sales beat from fragrances; risk: one quarter, and my sources give no full-year guidance). Lonza (case: FY26 outlook affirmed; risk: Q3 was only in line with expectations).
