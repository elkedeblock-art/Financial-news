# Weekly stock market news - 2 November 2026 (monthly sector edition)

## 1. Headline
- A tech-led sell-off on Friday 30 October hit US stocks after weak AI-related earnings: the Nasdaq fell about 2.0% on the week and had its worst month since March 2025.
- Europe and Switzerland held up better. The STOXX 600 rose about 1.7% and the SMI gained about 2.4%, helped by defensive heavyweights and an ECB that held rates.
- Inflation and rates are still the backdrop. The Fed held at 3.75–4.00% under Chair Kevin Warsh, and the 10-year US yield sits near 5%.

## 2. Switzerland
- **SMI:** It closed Friday at 14,810.66, up about 2.4% on the week and about 11.6% year to date (MarketScreener).
- **Defensives lead:** The SMI rose 4.6% in October to 23 October, while the Euro Stoxx 50 fell 3.2%. That is the widest gap since March 2020. Roche, Novartis and Nestlé drove it, in a shift towards defensives (Bloomberg via Yahoo, GuruFocus).
- **Company news:** UBS and Sandoz fell after results on 28 October. Roche rose on a positive lung cancer trial result. Nestlé deconsolidated its Russian business.
- **Economy:** The KOF Economic Barometer fell in October.
- **SNB and CHF:** The SNB policy rate is 0.00%. EUR/CHF was about 0.92 and USD/CHF about 0.82–0.825 on 30 October. Sources differ slightly on the exact USD/CHF level.

## 3. Europe
- **Weekly moves:** The STOXX 600 closed at 646.75 (+1.7%). The DAX rose 2.5% to about 25,818. The CAC 40 rose 2.4% and the FTSE 100 rose 0.4%. I did not find a confirmed Euro Stoxx 50 weekly figure.
- **ECB:** It kept its benchmark rate unchanged on 29 October. The STOXX 600 rose 1% that day, its biggest one-day gain in over a month. Strong results from Shell and Adidas helped.
- **Risk:** European tech also fell sharply on Friday. Chip names such as ASM International remain sensitive to AI headlines.

## 4. United States
- **Weekly moves:** The S&P 500 closed at 7,580.07 (−0.6%). The Nasdaq Composite closed at 25,437.00 (−2.0%). The Dow closed at 51,770.31 (−0.9%). Small caps (Russell 2000) rose 1.3%.
- **Friday:** The S&P fell about 1.8% and the Nasdaq about 2.9%. Sources differ by a few tenths on the daily figures. The S&P is about 3% below its 6 October record of 7,819.
- **Earnings:** Intel guided light and fell about 29%. Alphabet posted negative free cash flow and had its worst day on record. Meta also disappointed. FactSet still projects Q3 S&P 500 earnings growth of about 30%.
- **Fed:** The Fed held rates. DoubleLine's Jeffrey Gundlach said it should be raising them. Another manager thinks markets price in too many hikes.

## 5. Rates, currencies and commodities
- **Yields:** The US 10-year was around 5.0% around the Fed meeting, after touching about 5.35% in early October, its highest since 2002. The 2-year was about 4.7%. I found no Swiss or German 10-year figures except that the Swiss Confederation yield was 0.505% on 30 October.
- **Oil and gold:** Brent was around $109–114 and WTI about $100 during the week, amid Gulf supply worries. Gold rebounded above $4,000 on 26 October.
- **Asia:** India's Nifty 50 rose 2.0% on the week.

## 6. How the market is evolving
- **Rates cycle:** The Fed raised rates in September and is debating further hikes, with US inflation reported at 3.7%. The ECB and SNB are on hold, so policy is diverging.
- **AI spending vs. returns:** Analysts say AI cash needs are now worrying investors. DWS flags large AI capex. Hyperscaler results hurt the "Magnificent Seven" this week.
- **Rotation:** In October, staples, healthcare and utilities rose while tech and communication services lagged. The equal-weight S&P 500 beat the cap-weighted index for a fourth week.
- **Disagreement on tech:** State Street sees resilient tech earnings revisions. Others, such as BTIG, warn the S&P is vulnerable to a sell-off. Vance Roberts is "slightly bullish" for a bounce.
- **Risks:** Oil near $100+, bond yields near 5%, US midterms, and concentrated index leadership.

### Technology & semiconductors
Analysts expect the strongest 2027 EPS growth here (about 41%, Empower), driven by AI demand. The risk is that AI spending outpaces revenue, as this week's sell-off showed.
- **ASML (ASML / ASML.AS), Europe:** It is a key supplier of chip-making equipment. The risk is that chip stocks swing sharply on AI and China-competition news.
- **iShares Global Tech ETF (IXN), global:** It gives broad exposure. The risk is heavy concentration in a few mega-caps.

### Healthcare & pharma
2027 earnings growth is projected at about 22% versus about 17% for the S&P 500 (CFRA, Empower). Risks are pricing policy and a crowded defensive trade.
- **Roche (ROG), Switzerland:** It has a diversified drug and diagnostics pipeline. The risk is trial setbacks after a strong run.
- **Novartis (NOVN), Switzerland:** It is focused on innovative medicines. The risk is patent expiries.

### Financials
Higher yields can support bank earnings, and Julius Baer sees value in European banks. The risk is credit stress or sudden rate moves.
- **UBS (UBSG), Switzerland:** It is a global wealth manager. The risk is capital rules and share weakness after results.
- **JPMorgan Chase (JPM), US:** It is a large, diversified bank. The risk is a credit cycle downturn.

### Industrials & automation
Improving manufacturing PMIs support the sector, and CFRA expects improvement. Risks are the cyclical slowdown and about $240bn of debt due 2027–29 across the sector.
- **ABB (ABBN), Switzerland:** It is a leader in electrification and automation. The risk is an industrial downturn.
- **Siemens (SIE), Europe:** It is exposed to automation and infrastructure. The risk is slowing orders.

### Energy transition & utilities
Grid upgrades, storage and gas generation may create growth. Schwab rates utilities less favoured, and the sector has high leverage and sensitivity to 5% yields.
- **Schneider Electric (SU), Europe:** It is an energy-management company. The risk is a rich valuation.
- **iShares Global Clean Energy ETF (ICLN), global:** It is a diversified theme fund. The risk is rates and policy support.

### Consumer staples
The sector is defensive and attracted flows in October, with about +9% that month (CFRA). Growth and margins are lower, and the sector has higher leverage.
- **Nestlé (NESN), Switzerland:** It is a global food brand portfolio. The risk is weak volume growth.
- **Procter & Gamble (PG), US:** It has pricing power in household goods. The risk is input costs.

### Consumer discretionary & luxury
Analysts see a −2.4% 2027 EPS estimate, which is the weak spot. Higher rates and oil prices squeeze spending.
- **Richemont (CFR), Switzerland:** It is a luxury jewellery and watch group. The risk is demand swings and a possible De Beers bid, reported as under discussion.
- **Amazon (AMZN), US:** It combines retail and cloud. The risk is that it was in Friday's tech sell-off.

### Energy & materials
Oil near $100 supports current profits, but 2027 EPS is expected to fall about 9%. Swings in oil and Gulf politics drive results.
- **Shell (SHEL), Europe:** It reported strong results this week. The risk is a fall in oil prices.
- **Amrize (AMRZ), Switzerland/US:** It is a building-materials company that narrowed its 2026 revenue guidance to $12.2–12.4bn. The risk is construction cyclicality.
